There Is No Way To Phone You And No Form To Fill In. Your Whole Website Is 430 Words.

0
ways to contact you from your website

You are paying another company for jobs because your own website cannot produce one.

You told Santiago you pay Cosmic Solar $400 or more per job for work in San Diego, and that you want two more jobs a week, closer to San Jacinto.

Your website has no clickable phone number, no enquiry form, and 430 words across five pages. It cannot bring you a single job in its current state.

Every page read individually, 25 September 2026
Finding 1 — Nobody Can Contact You
Tappable phone numbers 0 Enquiry forms 0 Every page, measured: / 109 words /about 96 /contact 95 /gallery-1 90 /cart 40 TOTAL 430 words, 5 pages
This is the whole audit in one line

Your contact page is 95 words and there is no form on it and no number that dials when tapped. A homeowner who finds you, likes what they see and wants a quote has nothing to do next.

Everything else in this document is secondary to that. There is no point improving anything on a website that cannot take an enquiry.

And there is a /cart page. That is a shopping-cart page from the template the site was built on. You do not sell products from a cart, and it should not be there.

Finding 2 — Nothing Is Measured
Google Analytics not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found
Which means you cannot answer the question that matters most

You are paying Cosmic Solar per job. The single most useful number in your business would be what a job costs you from them versus what one would cost from your own marketing.

You cannot currently calculate the second half of that. Analytics is free and takes minutes, and until it is on, every decision about where to get work is a guess.

Finding 3 — The Jobs You Want Are Nearer Than The Jobs You Buy
Where your bought jobs are: San Diego Where you want to work: San Jacinto and closer Distance, San Jacinto to San Diego: roughly 80 miles
You are paying to drive

You are paying $400 or more per job for work eighty miles away, and you want work closer to home. Those two facts sit badly together — the fee buys you a job, and then the distance costs you a day.

Riverside County is your own back yard and there is no page on your site about it. Not San Jacinto, not Hemet, not Perris, Menifee, Moreno Valley or Temecula. Nothing local at all.

This is the gap between the business you have and the business you said you want, and it is a website problem before it is an advertising problem.

Finding 4 — The Hiring Problem Is On The Website Too
You said you struggle to find licensed or qualified people

There is no careers or hiring page on your site. A licensed solar installer or electrician looking for work in Riverside County cannot find out that you are hiring, what you pay, or what the work is like.

Hiring is marketing. The same page that convinces a customer you are a real, established company convinces an installer that you are worth working for — and right now there are 430 words for either of them to read.

Worth discussing what licences you actually need a candidate to hold, because that changes whether this is a recruitment page or a training-and-progression page.

What Is Already Right
There is a foundation here, it is just very small
  • LocalBusiness and WebSite structured data are present. That is more than many small contractors have, and it is the correct markup for what you do.
  • The site loads properly and is not broken. Nothing here is damaged — it is unfinished.
  • You have a gallery page. Solar is bought on proof, and a gallery is the right instinct even if there are only 90 words supporting it.
  • You own the domain and the site. Everything below is adding, not rebuilding.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Put a phone number and a form on every page. Today, before anything else. Nothing on this list matters until somebody can reach you.
  • Put analytics on, so you can start comparing the cost of a Cosmic Solar job against the cost of your own.
  • Build the Riverside County pages — San Jacinto, Hemet, Perris, Menifee, Temecula, Moreno Valley. That is where you said you want the work.
  • Write the service pages — install, battery, repair and service, commercial. Google cannot show a page that does not exist.
  • Add a hiring page, and tell installers what the work and the progression look like.
  • Remove the /cart page and anything else left over from the template.
  • Then advertise — once there is somewhere for the clicks to land and a way to count them.

You are buying jobs eighty miles away because your website cannot find you one next door.

Four hundred and thirty words, no phone link, no form. This is the cheapest problem you will ever fix.
