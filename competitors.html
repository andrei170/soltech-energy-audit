Your Rivals Publish 39,000 Words Each. You Publish 430. But Not One Of Them Speaks Spanish.

430
words on your entire site, against roughly 39,000 on each rival

We are not going to soften this. On published content you are the smallest site in your own market by a factor of about ninety.

But two things in this comparison are genuinely in your favour, and both are cheap to take.

Nobody in this market publishes anything in Spanish. Not one page, not one line, on any of the three firms we checked. And one of the two biggest is running analytics that stopped collecting data in July 2023 — they have been flying blind for over three years.

Finding 1 — Nobody In This Market Publishes A Word Of Spanish
Checked each firm's homepage and published sitemap for: "español" / "se habla" / "hablamos" / a /es/ URL / lang="es" Option One Solar none found Barnes Solar none found (7 sub-sitemaps, no Spanish URL) Progressive Energy none found (6 sub-sitemaps, no Spanish URL) SolTech Energy you and your crew speak it
This is the most valuable thing you own and it is currently invisible

You are selling solar in Riverside County, where a very large share of households speak Spanish at home. Solar is a considered purchase involving finance agreements, roof access, permits and a twenty-year warranty. It is exactly the kind of decision people prefer to make in the language they are most comfortable in — especially when someone is asking them to sign a finance document.

Every competitor we checked is invisible to that buyer. Not because they chose to be. Because it did not occur to anyone.

Your website currently does not say you speak Spanish either. So right now you have the advantage and none of the benefit. A Spanish version of your main pages, and a single line on the homepage, would make you the only firm in this comparison that person can read.

We have flagged in your concept site that any Spanish copy we draft must be reviewed by a native speaker before it goes anywhere near a live site. Bad Spanish would do more harm than none.

Finding 2 — Barnes Solar Has Been Flying Blind Since July 2023
Barnes Solar (barnessolar.com) Analytics found UA-223483055-1 UA-87883415-1 GA4 none found Tag Manager none found Enquiry forms 28 Tappable numbers 1 Homepage title "Orange County Solar Companies..."
Universal Analytics stopped processing new data on 1 July 2023

Those UA- tags are Google's old analytics product. Google switched it off for good in July 2023. It is still sitting in their pages doing nothing. They have 28 enquiry forms and no working way of knowing which one anybody fills in, or which of their 28 pages produced it.

There is a second thing worth noticing. Their homepage title leads with "Orange County", not Hemet or San Jacinto. They serve this valley but they are not really of it. You are. On local work that is a genuine difference, and it is the one thing a national or regional installer can never take from you.

The point for you is not that they are weak. It is how cheap the measurement gap is to close. You have no analytics at all, which is worse than theirs. But you are starting from nothing and can install the current thing correctly in an afternoon, while they have a dead tag nobody has noticed in three years.

Finding 3 — Option One Is The Real Competitor, And Here Is What They Do
Option One Solar (optiononesolar.com) Pages 30 Words ~39,000 GA4 + Tag Manager both live Enquiry forms 14 FAQ structured data present Meta pixel not found Spanish not found Their biggest pages: 3,027 /customer-support 2,875 /incentives 2,314 /commercial-solar-carports 2,226 /commercial-solar
Look at what their two biggest pages are about

Their single largest page is customer support, and the second is incentives. Not "why choose us". They have worked out that the two things a solar buyer is actually anxious about are what happens after the install, and whether the tax credits and rebates are real.

You have no page on either. You have no service pages at all — no install page, no battery page, no repair page, no commercial page. That is five pages that would each be doing work for you, and none of them requires you to be a writer. You explain this to customers on the phone every week.

They also have two commercial pages and you have none, which is worth raising because commercial solar is bigger tickets and fewer competitors.

What they do not have: a Meta pixel, or a word of Spanish.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Put a phone number and a form on the site. You currently have neither, anywhere. Everything else on this list is worthless until someone can contact you. This is the single most urgent item in either document.
  • Install analytics. You have none. An afternoon's work, and until it is done every other decision here is guesswork.
  • Write five service pages — install, battery, repair and service, commercial, and what happens after the install. Option One's biggest page is customer support; that is not an accident.
  • Add a page on incentives and rebates. Their second biggest page. It is the question every solar buyer asks and nobody local has answered it in Spanish.
  • Then do the Spanish version, reviewed by a native speaker. This is the one thing on the list that makes you the only firm in the market rather than a better version of one.
  • Build town pages for the valley — San Jacinto, Hemet, Menifee, Perris, Moreno Valley. You currently have no page for San Jacinto, which is where you are.
  • Remove the /cart page. It is left over from the template and you are not selling anything from a basket.

You are a long way behind on the site, and standing on the one piece of ground nobody else has touched.

The gap in content is real and we are not going to pretend otherwise. But it is all buildable, and the Spanish advantage is yours whether you use it or not.
